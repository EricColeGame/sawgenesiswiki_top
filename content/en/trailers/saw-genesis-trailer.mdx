## How to Tell a Real Saw Genesis Trailer From a Fan-Made Fake

This is the most useful skill you can develop right now. Fan-made trailers have gotten genuinely good, and generative tools have made convincing fake footage cheap to produce. Use the table below as a quick screening test.

| Signal | Fan-Made Warning Sign | Official Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Upload source | Unverified channel, generic name | Studio or distributor channel |
| Visual consistency | Shifting grain, warped hands, unstable faces | Consistent color grade and film texture |
| Audio | Reused score, clipping, mismatched levels | Licensed cues, professionally mixed |
| Billing block | Missing, misspelled, or invented credits | Full legal billing block |
| Legal card | Absent | Copyright line and rating card |
| Description | Vague, no official links | Links to studio pages and press assets |

### A Five-Minute Verification Routine

1. **Check the channel.** Official trailers appear on studio or distributor accounts, not on a channel created three weeks ago.
2. **Look for the rating card.** A green or red band before the footage is a strong indicator of a legitimate theatrical trailer.
3. **Reverse-search key frames.** If a shot appears in an older film, it's recycled.
4. **Cross-check the news.** If a trailer were real, trade outlets would cover it the same day.
5. **Read the description.** Real uploads link to official pages; fakes link to reaction videos or nothing at all.

If a clip fails two or more of these checks, treat it as fan-made until proven otherwise.
